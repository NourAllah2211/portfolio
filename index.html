Tutoring Marketplace

An app and responsive website where parents find and book the right tutor, and tutors run their teaching in one place.

UX researcher and designerFigmaMobile + webEgypt market
The briefDesign an app and responsive website where tutors list services and parents search and book.
The problemParents pick tutors by word of mouth and can’t see quality. Tutors are matched by age and lose time to admin and cancellations.
The solutionLevel-based matching, proof-led tutor profiles, side-by-side comparison, and one dashboard for tutors.

All problem statements and user stories

Problem statements and user stories

Parents

Fatima needs a reliable way to discover and evaluate tutors, because today she relies on recommendations and can’t see quality or results before booking.

She needs a tutor who adapts to each child, because it’s hard to find one who understands abilities and learning style.

She needs a tutor who knows the updated curriculum and exam system, because many only explain material.

She needs effective online sessions, because they lose value when engagement isn’t managed.

She needs easier scheduling, because conflicts push her to pick a convenient tutor over the best one.

Tutors

Tutors need to understand a student’s level and needs before starting, because matching by age hurts teaching.

Tutors need a reliable scheduling system, because last-minute cancellations waste preparation time.

Tutors need to show their experience and results, because trust is hard to build before booking.

Tutors need tools to keep students engaged online.

Tutors need one organized place for content, student info, and progress, because many tools add workload.

Parent stories

  • As a parent, I want to compare tutors by experience and teaching style, so I can choose the right one.
  • As a parent, I want to see a tutor’s impact on previous students, so I can choose based on results, not only recommendations.

Tutor stories

  • As a tutor, I need ready-made content so I can spend more time teaching.
  • As a tutor, I need one platform for students, schedules, and communication so I save admin time.
  • As a tutor, I want to open or block time slots so I control my schedule.

CliniFlow

A clinic operations dashboard where front desk, doctors and admins share one live view of the day — and each role gets the actions it needs, one click away.

UX/UI designerDesktop-first SaaSResponsiveDesign system
The briefDesign a clinic management dashboard for the staff who run a clinic’s day: receptionists, doctors and administrators.
The problemBookings live on paper and spreadsheets, the queue lives in the receptionist’s head, and doctors step out to ask who is next.
The solutionOne live overview, one-click check-in, a “call next patient” flow for doctors, and role-based access to everything else.

Context

3user roles
5appointment statuses
8design decisions from pain points
AAcontrast across the UI
One shared, live view of the clinic’s day.

CliniFlow is an enterprise tool, not a patient booking app. Its users are on their feet, interrupted every few minutes, and switching between a paper book, the phone and chat apps. The design had to make the most frequent actions — finding a patient, checking them in, moving the queue — take seconds.

How might we…

  • …let the front desk see the whole day and the live queue at a glance?
  • …help doctors move to the next patient without leaving the room?
  • …give admins trustworthy daily numbers without manual reports?

The people

Proto-personas · built from role mapping and assumptions, to be validated with clinic staff

Mona, 31 · Front desk

“Everyone asks me how long they’ll wait, and I’m guessing.”

  • Check patients in within seconds
  • Give honest wait times
  • Stop juggling book, phone and WhatsApp

Dr. Karim, 44 · Pediatrician

“I lose minutes walking out to ask who’s next.”

  • Know the next patient and visit reason
  • Finish the session on time
  • Skip admin tools he doesn’t need

Hana, 38 · Clinic admin

“Every Sunday I rebuild last week’s numbers by hand.”

  • Track attendance and no-shows
  • Balance doctor schedules
  • Control who can change what

More decisions

  • Five fixed statuses, each with colour, dot and text — never colour alone
  • Booked vs attended chart and patient mix, fed by the same statuses
  • Undo for reversible actions; a confirmation with a reason for cancellations

Accessibility

  • WCAG AA contrast for all text and buttons
  • 44 px minimum touch targets
  • Labelled inputs, real buttons, visible focus
  • Tables scroll in their card; cards on mobile

Waymark

A corporate travel platform where employees book trips, managers approve them and Finance reimburses expenses, all on one shared record. Each role sees a different experience of the same trip.

Product definitionRole-based UXApproval workflowsResponsive web app
The briefDefine and design a multi-role enterprise web app for business travel: booking, approvals, policies and expense reimbursement.
The problemTrips are booked over email, approved on chat, and paid back weeks later. Nobody can see where a request stands or whether it follows policy.
The solutionOne record per trip and per claim, an automatic policy check, approvals only where they add value, and a clear status for every role.

Context

4user roles with their own experience
3core cross-role workflows
10key screens designed in depth
7travel policy rules, soft or hard
One trip, four views, and every action moves it forward.

I started with a product definition, not screens: the roles and their permissions, the core journeys, and the states each record moves through. Only when those were agreed did I wireframe and design. The scenario is a fictional company, Meridian Group, with about 600 employees in Cairo, Dubai and Riyadh.

How might we…

  • …let employees book a trip that follows policy without reading the policy?
  • …ask managers to approve only the trips that need a human decision?
  • …get receipts verified and paid back in days, not weeks?

Roles and permissions

Proto-personas · built from role mapping and assumptions, to be validated with interviews

Omar, 29 · Employee

“I never know what the policy allows, or where my request is.”

  • Search and book trips
  • Track approval and booking status
  • Submit expenses with receipts

Laila, 41 · Manager

“Requests reach me on email and chat with half the context.”

  • Approve, reject or request changes
  • Review policy exceptions
  • First approval on expense claims

Sara, 35 · Travel Admin

“I check every booking against policy by hand.”

  • Set booking rules, soft or hard
  • See every exception in one list
  • Confirm approval delegates

Youssef, 38 · Finance

“Missing receipts, and spend reports built in spreadsheets.”

  • Verify receipts line by line
  • Return claims or approve reimbursement
  • Track spend by team

States before screens

Every record has a fixed set of states, and each state has one owner: the person who must act next. That owner sees the action; everyone else sees the status. Each state is shown as a label, a colour and a step tracker, never colour alone.

Changes requestedEmployeeEdit and resubmit; the request keeps its historyThe manager’s comment

Expense claim states
Draft→Manager review→Finance review→Approved→Reimbursed
  • Manager or Finance can return a claim; only flagged lines open for editing
  • After a Finance return, a minor correction goes straight back to Finance; a material one (lines added or removed, or the total moves by more than EGP 500 or 10%) goes to the manager again

How I solved it

Employees don’t know the policyPolicy tags on every flight and hotel, with the reason
Managers approve everythingSmall in-policy trips are approved automatically
Exceptions lack contextReason required, shown next to the cheapest in-policy option
“Where is my request?”A step tracker and a notification at every hand-off
Whole claims sent backFinance flags single lines; only those can be edited
Edge cases designed for
  • A hard rule (business class on domestic flights) shows the option disabled with the rule, so search never feels broken
  • A manager on leave: approvals route to a delegate the Travel Admin confirms
  • A manager’s own trip goes to their own manager
  • Cancelling a confirmed trip shows the fee before confirming
  • Empty, error and validation states on every list and form
